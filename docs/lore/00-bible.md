# Hollow Wake: The Bible

## What this is

Hollow Wake MMO is the working title of a massively multiplayer action role-playing game set in a world called Aureth. This file is the first page of its lore. It says what the world is, how it should feel, and where to read next. Everything in the other files under this folder follows from the facts set here. Where a later file disagrees with this one, this one wins.

## The world in one breath

Aureth was woven. Everything in it, from a mountain to a mood, is a Pattern made of nine Threads on a Loom that spans all of reality. Whoever set that Loom is no longer present, and the world still bears the marks of their deliberate hand: stars that sit in rows, rivers that lie across the land in long laid curves, a staircase of fused glass that climbs a cliff to nothing. Three centuries ago something tore the Loom. Nobody agrees on what. The tear is called the Unraveling, and the world is still living in its aftermath: places where Threads tangled and never settled, which shift and regrow and are never quite the same twice; wellsprings where the Loom surfaces and can be touched; and more and more children born with a loose Thread in them, able to reach into the cloth of the world and change it.

Those people are Weavers. You are one of them. You arrive in the Weftmarches, a frontier where the cloth of the world is loose enough to be worked, and you make your way: by fighting through Knots that are never the same twice, by recovering what the old world left behind, by unpicking the Threads from what you find and binding them into gear, by sitting a vigil at a Fount until the land around it answers to you, by raising a Holding and setting its laws, and by growing your own Pattern, your Skein, into something the world has never seen.

## The pitch

A high-fantasy MMOARPG about weaving. The world is cloth, magic is weaving, crafting is reweaving, dungeons are tangles, and territory is a wellspring you have to earn the right to touch. Nine Threads give the game its elements, its damage types, its months and its folk religion. Players begin on a frontier that is being settled for the second time, among factions who disagree about what a Weaver owes the world, and they grow in power by growing the complexity of their own Pattern.

## Tone, in one page

Numinous. The world is holy in the old sense: it is more than it needs to be, and it knows you are small. Light moves under the water of a Fount like something breathing. A Landmark is quiet the way a cathedral is quiet. When players feel awe, the tone is right.

Old. Aureth has been here a long time and has been made and remade. There are roads nobody laid in living memory, and towers whose light has not gone out since before the tear. Age should be felt in the names, the ruins, the customs, and the way people are careful around things they do not understand.

Strange. The Loom was made by something that did not think the way people think. Firstworks have a logic that does not resolve. Knots obey rules that bend. Nothing should be ordinary that can be slightly askew: the stars are too regular, the sun is called the Shuttle because it crosses back and forth across the Warp, and the year has five days that do not belong to it.

Hopeful at the edges. This is not a dying world. The Mending held. Lanterns are being re-lit. New Founts wake every few years. Ninewells is a real town with real bread. Every Weaver is proof that the cloth can still be changed, and the frontier is a place people go to build, not only to loot. Hope lives in the margins, in what is being made, not in speeches.

Not grimdark. Horror is permitted and should be real, but it is never the point and never the last word. Blood is not texture. Cruelty is not wisdom.

Not cartoon. Nobody winks at the camera. Humour comes from people, not from the world making fun of itself. Names are real names.

The absent maker. The single most important feeling in Hollow Wake is that of walking through a house that was tidied by someone who then went out and did not come back. The table was set. The lamps were trimmed. The door is open. Whoever did this meant it, and they are not here. Everything in the world, from religion to architecture to the shape of the sky, should carry that feeling.

## The fixed facts

- The world is Aureth. Play begins in the Weftmarches.
- Reality is the Loom. Everything is a Pattern woven of Threads.
- There are exactly nine Threads: Ember, Tide, Storm, Stone, Bloom, Umbra, Lumen, Void and Mind. They are also the game's elements and damage types.
- People who can consciously alter Patterns are Weavers. A deliberate act of weaving is a Weave. A Glyph is a bound shape that changes how a Weave behaves. Crafting is Reweaving.
- The Unraveling tore the Loom roughly three centuries before the present. Its true cause is not known, and the lore must never settle it.
- Knots are places where Threads tangled in the Unraveling. They shift, regrow and are never the same twice. Landmarks are fixed, deliberately made places that survived.
- Founts are wellsprings where the Loom surfaces. Whoever binds a Fount can shape the land around it, raise structures, set decrees and draw its yield. A bound region is a Holding. Binding is a rite, not a conquest, though it can be contested.
- Callings are starting paths, never cages.
- A Weaver's personal Pattern is their HollowWake. Growing it is the long game.

## Why people go out

Settlements and factions need things done. Knots shed Thread-rich material and must be cut back before they spread. Lost Landmarks hold what the old world knew. What is recovered can be rewoven into gear. Founts that stand unbound breed Knots, so binding them is a civic act as much as an ambition. A Holding, once raised, needs feeding, defending and ruling. And beneath all of it, every Weaver feels the slow pull of their own Skein growing richer, and wants to know what it will become.
