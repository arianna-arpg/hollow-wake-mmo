# Regions: Aureth and the Weftmarches

## The shape of the world

Aureth is laid out like a bolt of cloth, and its peoples name its parts that way. South is the Hem: the warm sea along which Ilvary lies, the finished edge, the oldest settled coast. West is the Wake: the grey ocean the Wakefolk sail, scattered with islands, deepening toward the rumoured Selvage. North is the Girdle: the mountain range that holds the world's upper edge, where the Durn delve. East is the Fray: the torn country where the Unraveling opened and where the Last Stitch holds. Between the Girdle's southern foot and the Wake's eastern shore, north of Ilvary and west of the Fray, lies the Weftmarches.

Beyond these four edges, nobody knows. The Selvage, the world's own hem, is said to lie past the Wake. What lies past the Girdle is called the Fold and has not been crossed. What lies past the Fray is the Fray.

## The Weftmarches

The Weftmarches is the frontier. It was the Concord's Eastmarch once, a wet green borderland with a few Lanterns and many Founts, and it was emptied by the Unraveling and lay wild for two centuries before the Marchers came back into it. It is being settled for the second time now, by people who mostly have no seal, no boat and no seam, and it is the place in Aureth where the Loom is loosest: Knots are many, Founts wake readily, Weavers are born often, and the Patterns of ordinary things can be felt shifting underfoot like a floor that has not quite settled.

The name is a Marcher coinage, half joke and half warning: in this country the weft shows.

Its shape: the river Wend runs through it from the Emberfells in the north-east, down through the Hallows, and out to the Wake at the Saltwrack. The Loomroad, the Concord's old paved way, comes up from Ilvary in the south, crosses the Hallows at Candlehall and Ninewells, and runs on north toward Scarrow Gate, broken in places. The Sallows fill the low wet west between the valley and the coast. The Hush lies east of everything, where the land goes quiet before the Last Stitch.

Five sub-regions make up the Marches as the Compact counts them: the Hallows, the Sallows, the Emberfells, the Saltwrack and the Hush. Each has its own weather, its own dominant Threads, its own Founts and Landmarks, and its own kinds of Knot, which the local people name in their own way.

### The Hallows

The valley of the Wend: the settled heart of the Marches and the place where new Weavers begin.

Biome and look: temperate lowland, soft hills folded on one another like cloth on a table, hedgerows, water-meadows, old Concord orchards gone feral and new ones planted beside them, coppice woods, standing stones in fields. Chalk under thin soil, so the paths are white. Mist in the mornings off the river. Weather: four seasons, mild, with Tidespan rains that flood the meadows and a long gold Stonespan.

Mood: pastoral and old and faintly uncanny. Everything is tended, and everything was tended before by someone who is gone. A field has a barrow in it; the barrow has a door; the door is swept. The Spindle leans on its hill to the north-west of Ninewells and the whole valley seems to turn around it. The Hallows is the hopeful part of the Marches. Things grow here.

Dominant Threads: Bloom and Stone, with Lumen along the Loomroad and Umbra in the barrows.

Founts:
- The Wellmother, at the heart of Ninewells, where nine springs rise in a ring around a pool with light moving under it. Bound in common by the Nine Who Stayed and renewed every Selvage by the whole town. It cannot be contested; its Welling feeds the town's Heddles and the Hedge.
- Hobsmere, a Fount under a mere east of the town, Tide and Bloom, bound and lost and bound again by a succession of Marcher Holders; the classic first Holding, and the one the Compact uses to teach the Vigil.
- The Orchard Well, in the oldest of the feral Concord orchards, Bloom with a strain of Umbra, waking; its apples are not safe to eat and are prized by Reweavers.
- Candlehall Font, beside the re-lit waystation on the Loomroad south of Ninewells, Lumen, bound by the Chancery's Marchwarden under the Charter of Lanterns, which the Compact does not recognise.

Landmark: the Spindle. A Firstwork. A tower of pale stone on the hill called Spindle Knap, taller than any Lantern, leaning a little, with no door and no join in its stone and no top that anyone has seen clearly, because it is always slightly out of focus at the summit. Its shadow is the Marches' calendar: the Spindlewatch keep the count by it and declare the sixth Selvage day when the shadow falls behind. The valley's hills fold toward it. Birds will not perch on it. People sit at its foot to think, and find that they do.

Also in the Hallows: Candlehall, the Concord waystation on the Loomroad, re-lit by the Chancery and now its seat in the Marches; the steads of Ashdown and Fallow's Cross; Reedham on the Sallows-edge; the barrow-fields of the Marrick Downs.

Knots: Marchers name a Knot for the field it is in, and most Knots in the Hallows are small and old. Barrow-knots are Snarls in the burial mounds, Stone and Umbra, where something has woken that was laid down carefully; the Barrow at Fallow's Cross is the one every new Weaver is sent to first. Orchard Snarls are Bloom tangles in the feral orchards, where the trees have grown into one another and keep thornwights. Hobs are the Hallows' Tangles: a corner of a field that is always evening, a copse where it rains upward, a lane that comes out where it went in. They are mostly harmless and the children are warned off them anyway. Burls are rare here, and when one sets the Compact fences it and argues about it.

Ninewells is described in its own section below.

### The Sallows

The wet old forest west of the Hallows, between the valley and the sea.

Biome and look: a drowned wood. Willow, alder, black poplar and the lantern-oak, which is found nowhere else and whose leaves hold light after dark. Meres and channels, duckboard paths that rot and are re-laid, moss on everything, the water black and still. Moths the size of hands. Light comes down green and arrives late. Weather: wet, mild, misted; the Sallows has its own fog that does not lift with the sun.

Mood: hushed, green-dark, numinous and faintly menacing, and alive in every inch. The Sallows is where Bloom and Umbra are tangled together, and it is both the most living and the most decaying place in the Marches. People who love it love it hard. The Hospice is here, and so is most of what the Menders have learned about healing the Tangled.

Dominant Threads: Bloom and Umbra, with Tide in the meres.

Founts:
- Alderheart, a Fount in the roots of an alder so old it has become a grove, Bloom with Umbra beneath, bound by the Order of the Mended Thread, who raised the Hospice around it. Its Welling is sap that runs silver and heals.
- Mothwell, a dry well in a clearing where the moths gather, Umbra, unbound and wary; it has taken three Vigils and given none of them back.
- The Drowned Font, under the deepest mere, Tide, reachable only when the water chooses; Wakefolk flossers have bound it twice, for a season each time.

Landmark: the Sunken Lantern. A Concord Lantern tower, the one raised beside the Fount among the alders, which sank to its waist in the mere called Lanternmere, a mile from the Hospice, when the Loom tore. Its light did not go out. It burns green under the black water and can be seen for miles at night through the trees, and anything that has been under that light for long begins to grow where it should not. The Menders keep a boat there and go down on ropes. There are rooms below that are still dry.

Also in the Sallows: the Hospice at Alderheart; the duckboard road called the Long Walk; the moth-fields; the drowned stead at Old Reed.

Knots: the Sallows' people call its Knots Hollows, because they are usually inside something. A Hollow is a Snarl in a hollow tree or a drowned building, Bloom and Umbra, close and dark and full of what grew there; Old Reed Hollow is the best known, a lantern-oak so wide that a stead was built inside it before the tear. Mothlights are Umbra Tangles where the shadows have begun to detach and the moths will lead you in. Drownings are Tide Snarls under the meres, which flood and empty on a schedule that is not the tide's, and which hold the drowned, who are not kind. Burls in the Sallows are green and quiet, and the Menders bind them as gardens.

### The Emberfells

The uplands north and east of the Hallows, rising toward the Girdle.

Biome and look: fells, tors, scree and heather, granite with ember seams in it: veins of Flame Thread burning slow in the rock like coal seams on fire, so that the hillsides glow at night and snow never lies on certain slopes. Old Durn workings, spoil-heaps, drystone walls that go nowhere. Thin air and long views. Weather: wind always, hard winters, summer thunderstorms that stand on the tors for days.

Mood: hard, bright, windswept, sublime. This is the Marches at its most made: the Cinderstair climbs a scarp to nothing, the ember seams run in lines too straight for nature, and the Durn have been digging here since before the Concord and still speak of the stone as something that is being polite to them. People come here to get rich or to get quiet.

Dominant Threads: Stone and Flame, with Storm on the heights.

Founts:
- The Greyseam Heart, deep in the Durn workings below Scarrow, Stone, bound by the Greyseam Delvers and held by them through the tear and since; the oldest continuous binding in the Marches.
- The Kiln, a vent in the scarp face that breathes warm air and smells of a forge, Flame, bound and lost by half a dozen hands since the Marching; its Welling is twine that comes already spun.
- Skyfold, a Fount on the highest tor, Storm, dark since the tear until it woke in M 303 with a sound heard in Ninewells. Unbound. Everyone wants it.

Landmark: the Cinderstair. A Firstwork. A staircase of fused black glass, wide enough for six abreast, that climbs the great scarp above the Kiln in a single flight with no landing and stops in the air a hundred feet short of the top, with nothing above it and nothing below the last step but the drop. It is warm to the touch in all weathers. Climbing it is a rite for Marcher Weavers; standing on the last step is said to show you the Warp in daylight. Nobody has built on to it. Nobody has found where it was going.

Also in the Emberfells: Scarrow, the Durn delve-town at the Girdle's foot, and Scarrow Gate above it, the iron door into the mountain; the Loomroad's broken northern reach; the Kiln-camps of the Longwake Company's flossers; the tors called the Sisters, where the Storm Knots gather.

Knots: Delves are the Emberfells' Snarls, collapsed workings that the Loom has re-tangled into new galleries, Stone and Flame, held by cinderhounds and stoneboars and sometimes a Remnant that was wrought to guard a seam that no longer exists. The Delve at Slagtooth is the deepest known. Kilns are Flame Tangles, pockets of the scarp where the seam has come to the surface and burns in the open, and where ashlight gathers if the Knot has frayed. Skyfalls are Storm Thinnings on the tors, where lightning stands in the air and sound dies, and the Weaves cast there go further than they should. Burls in the Emberfells are the richest in the Marches, and the Delvers and the Company have gone to the Hedge over them.

### The Saltwrack

The western coast, where the Wend meets the Wake.

Biome and look: grey tidal flats, sea-stacks, dunes, salt-marsh, long shingle strands, and sea-caves that go further in than they should. Drowned Concord ruins that surface at low tide. Wakefolk moorings on the stacks. Wrack and driftwood everywhere, and the wind off the Wake that does not stop. Weather: storms, fog, bright hard days between; the tides are not reliable within a mile of a Knot.

Mood: grey, vast, lonely and beautiful. The sea here is the edge of the known, and the Wakefolk say the Wake is deep, and they say it in a way that makes landsmen look at the water. The Saltwrack is where Void is nearest in the Marches, under the Tide; it is also where the money is.

Dominant Threads: Tide and Storm, with Void beneath.

Founts:
- Saltheart, in a sea-cave under Greyheron Stack, Tide, bound by the Greyheron wake-house since before the Company's charter and the cause of half the Company's quarrels with its own people. Its Eye is only uncovered at the lowest tides.
- The Eye of Greyheron, at the foot of the Light, Storm and Tide, bound by the Light's keeper and by no one else, by a custom older than the Company.
- Lowkeel Eye, under the Company's counting-house at Lowkeel, Tide, bound by the Factor of the Longwake Company under a Chancery charter, with Writs that tax every cart that leaves the port.

Landmark: Greyheron Light. A Concord Lantern built as a lighthouse on a sea-stack off the coast, raised in C 731, whose beam still sweeps the Wake every night and has not missed one since it was lit. It is tended by a keeper of the Greyheron wake-house, a hereditary office that the Company has tried to buy and the Chancery has tried to charter, and neither has managed. The stair up the stack is cut in a spiral, the stack is hollow, and the Light's cellars go down below the sea.

Also in the Saltwrack: Lowkeel, the Company's port, the largest settlement in the Marches after Ninewells and nothing like it; the moorings on the stacks; the drowned Concord town of Mire's End on the flats; the dune-steads.

Knots: Wracks are the Saltwrack's Snarls, drowned Concord ruins that surface at the Knot's low tide and are entered by their roofs, Tide and Stone; the Wrack of the Nine Oars is the one the Company pays most for. Undertows are Tide Snarls in the sea-caves that flood on the Knot's own tide, not the sea's. Stillwaters are Void Thinnings on the flats and at sea, patches where the water does not move and a dropped stone makes no ring, and the Wakefolk will not row through them. Burls on the coast are salt-white, and the Wakefolk say they are pearls.

### The Hush

The eastern edge, where the Marches end and the Fray begins.

Biome and look: a high dry moor that goes quiet. Grass that does not move in wind. A sky in which the Warp is faintly visible by day, in lines. Sound arrives late here. Then the Last Stitch stands across the horizon, a line of needles taller than towers, and beyond it the Fray: a country with no fixed shape, where the Loom is open, which looks different from every angle and the same from none. Weather: none that is reliable. It can be evening at noon.

Mood: wrong and beautiful and very still. The Hush is where the Marches' hope and dread meet. The Menders hold the Last Stitch and it holds. The Loosed come and go across it. Weavers who go far into the Hush say that the Loom is louder there, and that they can hear it not being held.

Dominant Threads: Void and Mind, with Umbra at evening.

Founts:
- The Needle's Eye, at the foot of the central needle of the Last Stitch, Void and Mind, bound by no one. The Menders say it cannot be bound. The Loosed say it should not be. Vigils sat there do not end in the usual way.
- The Quiet Well, a Fount on the moor that answers only at the Selvage, Mind, bound for five days a year by whichever Weaver reaches it first, and unbound for the rest.

Landmark: the Last Stitch. A Mending work. A line of needles running north to south across the Fray's western edge, each taller than a Lantern tower, wrought of Lumen-stone and bound through with Bloom and Stone, planted by Benoic Solenne's company in M 71. The Fray has not widened past them since. The needles hum. The Menders keep a watch-house at the central needle, and a path runs along the line, which is the only safe road in the Hush. What the Stitch holds and how it holds it is not understood by the people who maintain it.

Also in the Hush: the Menders' watch-house at the Stitch; the Loosed camps, which move; the moor-road from Reedham; the Echo of Serapha, described below.

Knots: Echoes are the Hush's Thinnings, Mind, places that replay what happened in them: a stead that burns every evening, a conversation that has been going on since the tear. The Echo of Serapha, which replays an hour of a woman in Concord dress speaking to no one, is the one the Chancery would like to burn and cannot. Unmakings are Void Snarls, holes in the moor where the Loom shows through and the Unmade come out, and they are the most dangerous Knots in the Marches. Seams are Thinnings where the Warp shows in the ground as well as the sky, and where a Weaver can see their own Skein from outside. There are no Burls in the Hush. Nothing sets here.

## Ninewells

Ninewells is the town where new Weavers begin, and the safest place in the Weftmarches. It stands in the Hallows on the west bank of the Wend, on the Loomroad, half a day's walk north of Candlehall and in sight of the Spindle.

It was founded in M 226 by the Nine Who Stayed, nine Weavers of nine Threads who bound the Wellmother in common. Their names are on the nine wells that ring the town's heart: Hob Ashdown for Stone, Wenna Cole for Flame, Tamsin Reed for Tide, Piers Fallow for Bloom, Isolde Marrick for Lumen, Sable Grieve for Umbra, Bram Gale for Storm, Oriel Penrose for Mind, and the Ninth Well, which is Void's and has no name on it, because the ninth Weaver gave her name into the binding. Children are told that if you say your own name into the Ninth Well it will keep it for you, and they do not do it.

The Wellhouse stands over the Wellmother's pool: a round moot-hall of wrought stone, raised rather than built, where the Moot meets and where the Compact was sworn. Around it the town goes out in rings: the wells, then the Heddles and the market, then the houses, then the Hedge. The Hedge is the town's wall and is a living thorn-hedge woven up out of the Hallows' own Bloom, twice a man's height and as thick as a house, with gates that close at dusk. The Compact's militia is named for it, because standing at the Hedge is what the militia does, and so "the Hedge" means both the wall and the people who hold it.

What is in Ninewells: the Wellhouse and the Moot; the Hedge and its captain; the Heddle of Lowdy Burr, where Reweaving is taught; the Knotwives' house by the Fallow well; the Candle, which is the Chancery's clerk's office and is tolerated; the Longwake counting-house, which is resented; the Hospice-gate, a small Mender house by the north gate; inns, a smithy, a tannery, a school, and a graveyard outside the Hedge with a cord on every marker. The Spindlewatch keep a house on the Knap road and come in on market days.

Who is in Ninewells: Maud Thatcher, Speaker of the Moot, who holds the Compact together by stubbornness; Jory Fenn, Hedge-captain; Lowdy Burr, Heddle-mistress; Old Nance Hollins, the senior Knotwife, who ties knots for the whole valley and is not to be crossed; a Chancery clerk, a Company factor, a Mender sister, and whoever the Spindlewatch has sent down. Weavers of every Calling teach here, because the Compact pays them to.

What a new Weaver does here: learns to Reach, takes a Calling, is sent to the Barrow at Fallow's Cross, comes back, sells floss to Lowdy Burr, is asked by Maud Thatcher to do something about the Hob in the Ashdown bottom-field, hears Sev's name spoken in an inn and is told to forget it, and begins to understand that every power in the Marches would like to know what their Skein is going to become.

## The lands beyond

### Ilvary

The south. The Hem coast and the country behind it: the old Concord heartland, the richest and oldest and most orderly land in Aureth, and the Lantern Chancery's own. Cassevault is its capital, the city of lanterns, raised around Font Aurelle, whose First Lantern has burned since C 44 and did not go out on Ninth Void. Mirevault lies west along the coast, the Tide-house's city, where the Relighting began and where the Mending Houses have their mother-hospice. The Loomroads run between the cities, lit. The Vaults lie under them. The Founts of Ilvary are all chartered and all bound, by sealed Weavers who hold them on loan from the Chancery, and a Weaver without a seal in Ilvary is a Weaver with nothing.

Ilvary is Lumen's country: clear, lawful, exacting, old. Its people are called Ilvarene. It mourns the High Concord and distrusts the Marches, which are full of its unsealed children. It sends the Chancery, the Menders and the merchant houses north, and it does not send soldiers, because it has not yet needed to.

### The Wake and the Scatter

The west. The Wake is the grey ocean, and the Wakefolk hold it to be the wake of the maker's leaving: Tide at its surface, Void in its deep, and the Selvage somewhere past the horizon where the Long Sailing saw it. The Scatter is the archipelago in the Wake's eastern reach: islands named for what they look like from a boat, the Kettle, the Anvil, Greyheron's Rest, Halfmoon, Tallmast, where the wake-houses moor. The Wakefolk live in houses that are boats and boats that are houses, reckon descent through the mother's wake-house, and give to the Wake before every voyage. They sail by the Warp and sing wakesongs that are charts.

The Scatter is Tide's and Storm's and Void's. It lost half its people to the Stillwater and the Long Sailing and has not forgotten either. It trades floss and salt and fish with the Marches, sends its children to the Company's crews and its keepers to the Light, and does not entirely trust either.

### The Girdle and Ondur

The north. The Girdle is the mountain range that holds the world's upper edge, and the Durn hold that it is the hem that held when the Loom tore. Ondur is their city, delved into the Girdle's root behind Scarrow Gate, sealed through the Loose Years and open again now, cautiously. The Durn are Stone's own people, with Flame for warmth and Umbra for quiet: patient, blunt, deep-voiced, slow to decide and impossible to move once decided. They take a seam-name at adulthood from the working where they came of age, keep stone-silence about the maker, and trade Stone and Flame floss and worked iron for everything they do not grow, which is everything.

Past the Girdle is the Fold, and no one has crossed it. The Durn say there is nothing there, and say it in a way that means they have looked.

### The Fray

The east. The Fray is where the Loom tore, and it is still torn. From the Last Stitch it is a country with no fixed shape: a plain, a sea, a city, a sky with ground in it, depending on where you stand and whether you have blinked. The Unmade come out of it. The Loosed go into it and come back. Nothing else does. The Menders have held its western edge for two centuries and have never gone further than a day's walk in, and have not come back the same from that.

Whether the Fray is a wound, a mouth, a door, or a loom learning to weave itself is the whole argument of the present age.

### The Selvage

The world's edge, said to lie west past the Wake. The Wakefolk who returned from the Long Sailing say they saw it from a distance: a clean hem of light where the world is finished, not ragged, not broken, finished. No one has touched it. It is the one thing in Aureth that every people agrees was made on purpose.
