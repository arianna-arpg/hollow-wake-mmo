# Naming: Conventions and Example Lists

This file is for anyone who has to name something in Skein: a person, a place, a Weave, a Glyph, an item, a Holding, a Knot, a creature. The aim is that every name sounds like it comes from the same world and from the right part of it. Read the principles, find the culture, use the lists, and extend them in the same voice.

## Principles

Names are real names. They are pronounceable on first reading, specific rather than generic, and belong to a culture that has a sound. A name that could belong to any fantasy world belongs to none.

The Loom's own words are plain textile words used literally. Weave, Glyph, Fount, Knot, Skein, floss, twine, Heddle, Vigil, Welling, Writ, Reach, Snag, Burl, Selvage, Warp, Weft. When a new term is needed, take it from the same lexicon: shuttle, bobbin, hank, ply, hem, seam, stitch, needle, distaff, reed, cord, hitch, tie, nap, pile, fray, ravel. Do not invent a made-up language for the Loom; its vocabulary is ordinary and old, which is the point.

Each culture has a sound and the sounds do not mix. A Marcher has a Marcher name even if her grandmother was Ilvarene. A place keeps the name of the people who named it, so Concord works in the Marches carry the Common names the settlers gave them, and the Chancery's charters use older forms nobody else does.

Avoid: apostrophes inside names (possessive place names like Fallow's Cross are the only exception, and they are Marcher), diacritics, doubled consonants for the sake of strangeness, names borrowed from any real religion or people, anything that belongs to another fantasy property, and the words listed under Words to avoid at the end of this file.

Nine is the sacred number and is used sparingly. Most things come in other quantities.

## Marchers

The people of the Weftmarches: settlers and the children of settlers, of every descent, speaking the Common tongue plainly. Their naming is short, rural and earned.

People: a short given name of one or two syllables, old and plain, drawn from the same pool for women and men, and a by-name from a trade, a place or a feature. By-names are taken, not strictly inherited, though stead families tend to pass them on. Full names are given name then by-name: Maud Thatcher, Hob Ashdown.

Given names: Alys, Bram, Cass, Dunstan, Edda, Gil, Hesper, Hob, Isolde, Jory, Kit, Lowdy, Maud, Morwen, Nance, Oriel, Ottery, Piers, Rook, Sable, Tamsin, Tansy, Wat, Wenna.

By-names: Ashdown, Burr, Cole, Coppice, Fallow, Fenn, Gale, Grieve, Hedger, Hollins, Mallow, Marrick, Penrose, Reed, Rudd, Sparrow, Stook, Tallow, Tarr, Thatcher, Wende.

Places: compound Common words. Endings in -well, -mere, -combe, -down, -ham, -stead, -ford, -cross, -hallow, -knap and -bottom. Ninewells, Hobsmere, Ashdown, Reedham, Fallow's Cross, Spindle Knap, Marrick Downs, Old Reed, Tallowcombe, Wendford, Burrstead, Coppice Bottom.

Founts: a Marcher Fount is a well or a mere, named for where it is or what it does. The Wellmother, Hobsmere, the Orchard Well, the Quiet Well, Tallow Well.

Knots: named by kind and field, never grandly. The Barrow at Fallow's Cross, Old Reed Hollow, the Hob in the Ashdown bottom-field, the Snarl under Coppice Bottom.

Holdings: the Holder's by-name or the Fount's name, and Hold or Holding. Ashdown Hold, Hobsmere Holding, Fallow Hold.

Items: a plain noun, and if the item is famous, its owner's name and a quality. The Reed Coat, Nance's Cord, Hob's Patience (a hammer), Old Grieve's Lantern, the Thatcher Spear.

## Ilvarene

The people of Ilvary, the old Concord heartland. Their naming is long, soft, formal and inherited, with a sound that leans toward the Latinate and the courtly.

People: a given name of two to four syllables, soft consonants, open vowels, and a house name. House names are inherited and carry weight; the nine Concord houses are the oldest, and some of them are not borne by anyone now. Full names are given name then house, and a title goes before: Chancellor Isaure Castellane, Needle Oriane Solenne, Clerk Lucan Aurevant.

Given names: Amadee, Aurelis, Benoic, Caelis, Celandine, Elouan, Ferrant, Isaure, Lucan, Marisol, Mathis, Noemie, Ondrea, Oriane, Perrin, Serapha, Severin, Taelin, Vesper, Ysabeau.

House names, the nine of the Concord: Aurevant (Lumen), Belcastre (Mind; struck from the Lanterns, never borne by a living person), Castellane (Stone), Durenne (Flame), Mirevault (Tide), Solenne (Bloom), Valcoure (Storm; survives only as village names), Orriaux (Umbra), Quillane (Void; the quiet house, gone).

House names, lesser: Corbenne, Damiraux, Estrevant, Maurenne, Rosvault, Sallenge, Verrine.

Places: endings in -vault for a great city, -court, -enne and -mire for towns, and Font before a Fount's name. Cassevault, Mirevault, Rosvault, Estrecourt, Corbenne, Maurenne, Sallenge-on-Hem, Font Aurelle, Font Verrine. The Chancery's charters call Candlehall by its Concord name, Chandevault, which nobody in the Marches uses.

Founts: Font and a name. Font Aurelle, Font Verrine, Font Damiraux. In the Marches the Chancery says Candlehall Font and is corrected by nobody.

Weaves, formal names: an ordinal, a noun and a house, honouring the teacher who recorded the Weave. The Third Candle of Aurevant, the Fourth Tide of Mirevault, the First Needle of Solenne, the Seventh Door of Castellane. The common name is used in the field and the formal name in the schools.

Holdings: the Demesne of and the Fount's name. The Demesne of Font Aurelle, the Demesne of Candlehall.

Items: the noun of the house, or a Concord material. The Lantern-blade of Castellane, the Ledger of Aurevant, lantern-steel, vault-glass, roadstone.

Titles: Chancellor, Marchwarden, Lanterner, Clerk; Needle, Stitcher, Sister, Brother, Warden of the Stitch.

## Wakefolk

The people of the Wake and the Scatter, also called Shoalfolk by landsmen. Their naming is quick, vowel-ended, and tied to the boat.

People: a given name of two syllables ending in a vowel, built from a small set of consonants (v, r, l, sh, m, t, k, n, h), and a wake-house name, which is the name of the boat-house a person is born to, reckoned through the mother. Full names are given name then wake-house: Varu Longwake, Ilesh Greyheron, Seshe Lowkeel.

Given names: Anu, Hesha, Ilesh, Isho, Kelat, Lorue, Maelo, Maru, Nimo, Omeru, Orvi, Reshi, Seshe, Talu, Teshan, Tomaru, Ushani, Valesh, Varu, Veshi.

Wake-houses: Deepline, Greyheron, Gullcry, Halfmoon, Longwake, Lowkeel, Ninecurrent, Saltwife, Stormtithe, Tallmast.

Places: an island is named for what it looks like from a boat, and a mooring for the wake-house that keeps it. The Kettle, the Anvil, the Teeth, Greyheron's Rest, Halfmoon, Tallmast, Lowkeel.

Founts: an Eye, or a heart. The Eye of Greyheron, Lowkeel Eye, Saltheart, the Eye under the Kettle.

Weaves: named for currents, birds and weathers. Undertow, Riptide, Squall, Gullcry, Heronstep, Longswell, Spindrift.

Holdings: the wake-house and Mooring. Greyheron Mooring, Tallmast Mooring.

Items: birds and weather. Gullwing (a knife), the Squall Coat, Heron's Patience (a long oar), Saltwife's Needle.

Wakesongs: titled as routes. Kettle to Halfmoon by the Warp; Nine Days of Stillwater; The Hem Seen from the Teeth.

## Durn

The people of the Girdle and Ondur. Their naming is short, hard and taken from the stone.

People: a given name of one or two syllables with hard stops and clusters (dr, kr, br, th), vowels mostly a, o and u, and a seam-name taken at adulthood from the working where the person came of age. Full names are given name then seam: Haldrek Greyseam, Ysmur Firstdelve.

Given names: Benda, Drusk, Ebbe, Gudda, Haldrek, Hesk, Kadra, Korrin, Morrak, Oska, Rukk, Thorva, Tovek, Ulda, Varda, Ysmur.

Seam-names: Blacklode, Brightvein, Coldshaft, Deepcut, Firstdelve, Greyseam, Hollowgate, Ironfold, Slagtooth, Underlode.

Places: short, ending in -ur, -ow or -dr. Ondur, Scarrow, Kaldur, Hadrow, Thurn. The Girdle and the Fold are Common names the Durn accept.

Founts: the seam and Heart. The Greyseam Heart, the Coldshaft Heart.

Weaves: single hard words. Grip, Shale, Cinder, Hold, Crush, Bank, Flint, Slag.

Holdings: the seam and Delve. Greyseam Delve, Ironfold Delve.

Items: the seam and the tool, or, for famous things, the tool and what it did. Greyseam Pick, Coldshaft Lamp, the Pick that Opened Scarrow, the Door that Rang.

Titles: Seam-master, Delver, Gate-keeper.

## The Loosed

The Loosed take frayed names: the old name cut to one syllable and nothing added. Sev from Severin, Ond from Ondrea, Lis from Caelis, Ash from Ashlin, Tov from Tovek, Hal from Haldrek, Mar from Marisol. They name nothing else. They refer to places by what the Loom does there: where it is loud, the open, the quiet past the needles.

## Firstworks, Concord works and Mending works

Firstworks are named with a plain Common noun and the definite article, and never after a person: the Spindle, the Cinderstair, the Warp, the Fold, the Selvage, the laid rivers.

Concord works carry Lantern, Light, Road, Vault, Hall or End in their names. In Ilvary they keep Ilvarene forms; in the Marches they carry the Common names the settlers gave them. The First Lantern, Mirevault's Lantern, the Loomroad, the Vaults, Greyheron Light, the Sunken Lantern, Candlehall, Mire's End.

Mending works are named for what they do: the Last Stitch, the Hospice, the watch-house.

## Weaves

A Weave's common name is one plain word, or two run together, and names what the Weave does or looks like. No "of", no numbers, no adjectives on their own. The Thread is usually audible in the name but not always. Formal Ilvarene names are given above.

Flame: Kindle, Emberlash, Ashfall, Cinderedge, Hearthwall, Brand, Flare, Scorch, Kilnbreath, Emberstep.

Tide: Undertow, Hoarfrost, Coldedge, Tidewall, Ebb, Riptide, Snowfall, Stillwater, Longswell, Brine.

Storm: Crackle, Skystep, Galecry, Squall, Forklight, Thunderclap, Windrow, Stormstep, Heronstep, Whistle.

Stone: Rootstand, Bulwark, Shalesnare, Barrowgrip, Grip, Shale, Crush, Flint, Groundswell, Wallward.

Bloom: Quicken, Mend, Sapflow, Thornwall, Burgeon, Greenhand, Rootbind, Briar, Blossom, Harvest.

Umbra: Duskveil, Hollowing, Shadowstep, Mothlight, Rot, Lull, Gloam, Wane, Shroud, Nightfall.

Lumen: Lanternward, Lanternmark, Clarity, Dawnline, Glare, Candle, Beam, Verdict, Trim, Lightfast.

Void: Unname, Unpick, Blink, Null, Gap, Unmake, Lapse, Silence, Closedeye, Drop.

Mind: Read, Rouse, Hitch, Recall, Remember, Name, Dream, Will, Ken, Insight.

## Glyphs

A Glyph is named for its shape, with the definite article, and the shape says what it does to a Weave. Weavers speak of them as things tied in: "Kindle with a Fork and a Burr."

The Fork (splits), the Ring (spreads), the Anchor (holds in place, lasts), the Echo (repeats), the Needle (pierces), the Burr (clings, lingers), the Hook (pulls), the Loop (returns), the Ply (strengthens), the Twist (changes the Thread), the Ladder (builds), the Selvage (adds an ending), the Shuttle (passes from one to the next), the Slipknot (releases early), the Hem (shortens, finishes clean), the Bobbin (stores and lets go), the Reed (spaces evenly), the Cross (two Threads together).

## Items

Ordinary items are material and noun: an ash spear, a tin lamp.

Rewoven items carry the Thread they were wrought in: emberwrought, tidewrought, stormwrought, stonewrought, bloomwrought, duskwrought (for Umbra), lanternwrought (for Lumen), hollowwrought (for Void), silverwrought (for Mind). An item with room in its Pattern for Glyphs is loose-wove; one with none is close-wove.

Materials from the Loom: floss and twine by Thread (Flame floss, Tide twine), Burl floss for the rare kind, fray-floss for what the Loosed bring out of the east. Concord materials: lantern-steel, vault-glass, roadstone.

Famous items are named by their culture's rule above. Wherever possible a named item has a story that fits in one sentence.

## Holdings

A Holding is named by its Holder at the Vigil, and the name is wrought into the Fount; it cannot be changed without a new Vigil. Forms by culture: Hold or Holding (Marcher), the Demesne of (Ilvarene), Mooring (Wakefolk), Delve (Durn). A common-bound Holding takes the Fount's name and Reach: the Wellmother's Reach. Nobody in Aureth says kingdom, realm or empire, and the Chancery says demesne with a straight face.

## Knots

By kind in general speech: Tangle, Snarl, Thinning, Burl. By local name in each region: Hobs, Barrow-knots and Orchard Snarls in the Hallows; Hollows, Mothlights and Drownings in the Sallows; Delves, Kilns and Skyfalls in the Emberfells; Wracks, Undertows and Stillwaters on the Saltwrack; Echoes, Unmakings and Seams in the Hush. A particular Knot is the kind at the place: the Delve at Slagtooth, the Wrack of the Nine Oars, the Echo of Serapha.

## Creatures

The Tangled are named by Thread and beast, in one word where the word will bear it: cinderhound, stoneboar, thornwight, hollowman, glassling, ashwolf, barrowman, mothhollow; the drowned are only ever the drowned. The Unmade have no names and are not given any; one says an Unmade, or the Unmade of a place. Remnants are named by what they were wrought to do: roadwarden, seam-keeper, lanternbearer, doorkeeper.

## Factions and titles

The Lantern Chancery: Chancellor, Marchwarden, Lanterner, Clerk; the Candle is its office in a town; its seal is the Lantern's seal.

The Weftmarch Compact: Speaker of the Moot, Speaker (of a stead), Hedge-captain, Heddle-master or Heddle-mistress, Knotwife.

The Longwake Company: the Factor, factor, wake-captain, flosser, hand.

The Order of the Mended Thread: the Needle, Stitcher, Sister, Brother, Warden of the Stitch.

The Loosed: no titles. The Chancery's word for them is ravelers.

The Greyseam Delvers: Seam-master, Delver. The Spindlewatch: Watcher. The Hedgecrows: Crowmother, crow.

## Words to avoid

These words belong to other games or to no world, and Aureth has its own: mana (a Weaver draws on their Skein), spell (Weave), wizard or mage (Weaver), dungeon (Snarl), guild (Company, Compact, Order), kingdom or realm (Holding, Reach, Demesne), god (there are none; say the Loomwright, or the culture's name for the maker), demon (the Unmade, or the Tangled), undead (the drowned, barrowmen, hollowmen), portal (Seam, Thinning), loot (floss, yield, Welling, what was recovered), boss (the Snag), element (Thread).
