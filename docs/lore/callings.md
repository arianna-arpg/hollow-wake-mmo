# Callings: The Six Paths

## What a Calling is

A Calling is the path a new Weaver starts on. It is the first shape their Skein is taught to take: a set of first Weaves, a way of Reaching, a tradition of teachers, and a lean toward two or three Threads. It is a beginning, not a boundary. The Marcher saying is that a Calling is the door you came in by, and nobody is asked to leave by it. Weavers cross. A Holdfast who takes up Bloom becomes something the Hallows has a word for, and a Shade who learns to Kindle is not an oddity, only a Shade with a warmer hand. Old Weavers' Skeins owe their shape to every Calling they have passed through, and the finest cannot be sorted into any.

How a Calling is taken: in Ninewells, by the Compact's custom, a new Weaver sits with a teacher of each Calling in the Wellhouse during their first days, Reaches under each one's hand, and takes the one that answered. Ilvarene schools assign by examination, and the Marchers find this funny. Wakefolk take a Calling at sea. The Durn take whichever their seam needs.

The six are given below in the voice of the Marches, each with the Threads it leans toward, the kind of weaving it does, who teaches it in Ninewells, and the shape of its first Weaves.

## Holdfast

The Holdfast is the one who stays. Where everyone else moves, the Holdfast plants their feet, draws Stone up through the soles and Lumen down through the crown, and becomes the thing that the fight happens around. They are shield and wall and door; they hold the line at the Hedge and the stair in a Delve and the ford while the cart crosses. A Holdfast's Weaves are slow and heavy and do not miss: ground that grips, skin that remembers being rock, a ward of clean light that turns a blow aside, and an Ember edge for when standing is not enough. They are the oldest Calling, older than the Concord, because someone always had to stand at the door. Holdfasts are patient, stubborn and often funny in a dry way, because they have had time to think. The Durn produce them by the seam, the Hedge makes them by necessity, and the Chancery's Lanterners are mostly Holdfasts who learned to read.

Threads: Stone and Lumen, with Ember.

Teacher in Ninewells: Jory Fenn, Hedge-captain, who teaches at the north gate and is not gentle.

First Weaves: Rootstand, Bulwark, Lanternward, Cinderedge.

## Kindler

The Kindler is the one who starts things. They draw Ember and Storm and throw them, and the world changes where the throw lands. A Kindler's Weaves are quick, bright and loud: a lash of fire, a crack of lightning, a gale that scatters, a burn that spreads. They lean on Mind more than they admit, because aiming is thinking, and the best Kindlers are the ones who can hold a Weave for a breath longer than seems wise and let it go at the right moment. They are impatient, generous and dangerous to stand next to, and they are the Calling most often blamed for things. The Concord's war-weavers were Kindlers; so were most of the Drawn, who could not hold on Ninth Void. In the Marches they are the flossers' best friends and the Hedge's first call, and they burn out young unless someone teaches them the Fall.

Threads: Ember and Storm, with Mind.

Teacher in Ninewells: Edda Cole, of Wenna Cole's line, who keeps the smithy and teaches in the yard behind it.

First Weaves: Kindle, Emberlash, Crackle, Ashfall.

## Sower

The Sower is the one who tends. They draw Bloom and Tide and give them away: a wound closes, a field stands up after hail, a friend who was falling finds the ground. A Sower's Weaves are patient, lasting and underestimated, because the Sower's work is to make everyone else's work possible, and there is no Sower's monument anywhere in Aureth. They lean toward Lumen when they need to keep something clean, and toward Umbra, uneasily, when something needs to be let go. Sowers are the Calling of midwives, hospices and farms, the Menders' own, and the one the Marchers trust most; a stead with a Sower in it survives. The hardest thing a Sower can learn is that Bloom does not ask whether, and the best Sowers are the ones who learn it early and keep tending anyway.

Threads: Bloom and Tide, with Lumen.

Teacher in Ninewells: Sister Celandine at the Hospice-gate, who teaches at the bedside.

First Weaves: Quicken, Mend, Sapflow, Hoarfrost.

## Shade

The Shade is the one who unpicks. They draw Umbra and Void, and Mind to aim them, and what they weave is the ending of things: a veil that is not seen through, a rot that spreads in an enemy's Pattern, a step through shadow, a word that takes a name off its owner. A Shade's Weaves are quiet and personal and arrive from behind. Shades are the Calling the close-woven are most afraid of and the one they most often send for, because secrets need keeping and some things need ending. They are discreet, dry and lonely, and they understand better than anyone that Umbra is not Void and that the difference is the whole of their craft. The Loosed recruit Shades first. The Chancery seals them last. In the Marches they are called unpickers, with respect.

Threads: Umbra and Void, with Mind.

Teacher in Ninewells: Sable Grieve's line has ended, so the Shade's teacher is whoever the Knotwives send, and this year it is a quiet Wakefolk woman called Seshe Lowkeel, who does not say why she left the Company.

First Weaves: Duskveil, Hollowing, Shadowstep, Unname.

## Harrier

The Harrier is the one who moves. They draw Storm into their feet and Tide into their breath, and they are where they were not: across the field, over the wall, behind the thing that is chasing them. A Harrier's Weaves are motion and distance: a step on the wind, a thrown edge of cold, a snare of Stone that the Harrier's own speed sets, a cry that carries. They are hunters, couriers, scouts and finders of lost Landmarks, the Calling most often alone and least often caught. Harriers are quick, restless, cheerful and not reliable about coming home, and the Wakefolk say the Calling was theirs first, because the first Harrier was someone who had to get off a boat in a storm and did. In the Marches they walk the Hush and the Sallows and the high tors, and they bring back the maps.

Threads: Storm and Tide, with Stone.

Teacher in Ninewells: Dunstan Gale, of Bram Gale's line, who teaches on the river path and will not stand still.

First Weaves: Skystep, Galecry, Coldedge, Shalesnare.

## Binder

The Binder is the one who ties. Where other Weavers work on the world, the Binder works on Patterns as Patterns: they draw Mind and Lumen and bind a small Pattern to their own, so that it stays, and acts, and answers. A Binder's Weaves raise a wrought thing from the ground, call a Tangled beast to heel, hold a ward in the air after the Binder has walked away, and read what a Knot is going to do before it does it. They lean toward Bloom when what they bind is alive and toward Umbra when it is not, and the Binders who go furthest learn to bind both. They are the Calling of the Vigil: Binders sit Founts best, hold Holdings longest, and are the first the Chancery seals and the first the Company hires. They are deliberate, curious and a little apart, and the Spindlewatch is mostly Binders who stopped binding and started watching.

Threads: Mind and Lumen, with Bloom or Umbra.

Teacher in Ninewells: Watcher Gil Penrose, on market days, at the Spindlewatch house on the Knap road.

First Weaves: Hitch, Lanternmark, Rouse, Read.

## Crossing

Every Calling has a teacher in Ninewells, and every teacher will take a Weaver of another Calling who asks, though not always kindly. Crossing is slow at first and faster later, because a Skein with more Threads in it takes new ones more easily. The Marches have names for some crossings. A Holdfast who takes up Bloom is a Hedgewright. A Sower who learns to Kindle is a Kilnwife or Kilnman. A Shade who learns to Bind is a Mothkeeper. A Harrier who takes up Umbra is a Crow. A Kindler who learns to hold is a Hearth. A Binder who learns to unpick is a Loosed, people say, and then look over their shoulder.

None of these are Callings. They are what people call you when your Skein has grown past the door you came in by, and the best Weavers in the Marches have been called several of them, and answer to none.
