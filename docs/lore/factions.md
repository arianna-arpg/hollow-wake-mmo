# Factions: The Powers in the Weftmarches

Five powers act in the Weftmarches, and three smaller groups live in the gaps between them. None of them is simply right. Each wants something defensible, each goes about it in a way that costs someone else, and each has a view of Founts and of Weavers that a new Weaver will be asked, sooner or later, to agree with.

The question underneath all of them is the same: what does a Weaver owe the Loom, and who gets to say?

## The Lantern Chancery

What it is: the old Concord's office of charters, restored at Cassevault after the tear, and the civil power of Ilvary. It keeps the Charter of Lanterns, seals Weavers, charters Founts, re-lights Lanterns, and regards all three as the same act. Its Threads are Lumen, Mind and Ember. Its symbol is a lantern. Its people are Chancellors, who sit at Cassevault and of whom one is sent north as Marchwarden; Lanterners, who are sealed Weavers in Chancery service and do its work in the field; and Clerks, who keep the Candle, the Chancery's office, in every town that will have one.

What it wants: the Charter of Lanterns extended over the Marches, so that every Fount is chartered, every Weaver sealed and every Lantern lit. It believes that the Loosening is the beginning of a second tear and that only order will hold the cloth, and it believes this sincerely. It wants the Vaults' lost knowledge recovered from the Eastmarch's ruins before the Company sells it. It wants the Loosed gone. Underneath, it wants to be the Concord again, and knows that it cannot.

How it works: law, patience and money. The Chancery does not conquer; it registers. It plants a Candle, offers seals to any Weaver who will swear, pays well, litigates endlessly, and refuses to recognise anything it did not charter. It re-lights Lanterns, which genuinely steadies the land around them and is the best argument it has. Its Lanterners close Knots competently and are respected for it. It has not sent soldiers, and the Marchwarden's standing instruction is not to be the one who does.

Founts: assets held on loan. The Marchwarden has bound Candlehall Font under charter, and the Chancery offers charters to Marcher Holders who will swear; some have taken them, and these are called sealed steads, and the Compact does not speak to them. The Chancery does not sit Unbindings. It issues writs of lapse and waits.

Weavers: licensed professionals, trained at the Lantern-schools, bound by the seal. An unsealed Weaver is a loose thread and a public danger, and the Chancery's whole doctrine follows from the belief that unlicensed weaving ended the world. Its strength is that it will seal anyone who asks and swears, of any people, and keep its word to them.

Seat and people: Candlehall, on the Loomroad south of Ninewells. Chancellor Isaure Castellane, the Marchwarden, old Concord blood, exact and tired and far from stupid; Lanterner Mathis Durenne, her second, who likes the Marches more than his office allows; Clerk Lucan Aurevant, who keeps the Candle in Ninewells and is polite to everyone.

How it sees the others: the Compact is a mob with a well; the Company is a useful vulgarity; the Menders are family it does not speak to; the Loosed are the end of the world walking.

## The Weftmarch Compact

What it is: the league of Marcher steads and towns sworn in the Wellhouse at Ninewells. Its Threads are Bloom, Stone and Tide. Its symbol is a knotted cord. It has a Moot, to which each sworn stead sends a Speaker and over which the Speaker of the Moot presides; a Hedge, which is its militia, named for Ninewells' living wall and made of every able adult; and its Heddles and Knotwives, who are not formally part of it and are the reason it works.

What it wants: the Marches free. The Founts as a commons, with the Vigil as the only title. No seals, no tolls, no writs that the Moot did not pass. Knots cut back before they spread. The Loosening to be what the Compact believes it is, which is the Loom healing. Underneath, it wants to prove that a people can hold a Fount in common and not be ruled by it, and it has one proof, and guards it.

How it works: assembly and oath. The Moot argues; the Hedge answers when a stead calls; steads lend to one another and remember who did not. It hires Weavers with the Wellmother's Welling, fences Burls and argues about them, boycotts the Candle, teaches the Vigil at Hobsmere to any Weaver who swears the Compact, and buys from the Hedgecrows what the Company's tolls would otherwise cost. It is poor, slow and stubborn, and it has never lost a Fount to a charter.

Founts: a commons. A Holder is a steward answerable to the Moot, not an owner, and the Compact will sit an Unbinding against a Holder it finds cruel or absent. It does not recognise Chancery charters or Company writs, and it treats the Wellmother's common binding as the model all other bindings should aspire to.

Weavers: neighbours with duties. A Weaver is expected to answer the Hedge, teach the young, cut the Knots nearest their stead, and tie in. The Marcher saying is that a loose-thread who will not tie in is a thread in the wind. The Compact pays its Weavers and does not license them, and every Calling is taught in Ninewells at its expense.

Seat and people: the Wellhouse at Ninewells. Maud Thatcher, Speaker of the Moot, who holds the Compact together by will; Jory Fenn, Hedge-captain, who has fought the Tangled, the Hedgecrows and the Company's flossers and prefers the Tangled; Lowdy Burr, Heddle-mistress, who taught half the Reweavers in the Marches; Old Nance Hollins, senior Knotwife, who is the Compact's conscience and knows it.

How it sees the others: the Chancery is the old world trying to come back; the Company is a thief with a receipt; the Menders are friends who will not pick up a spear; the Loosed are a fear it is ashamed of, because some of them are its own.

## The Longwake Company

What it is: a chartered trading consortium of Wakefolk wake-houses, Longwake, Lowkeel and Tallmast among them, in partnership with the Ilvarene merchant house of Orriaux. Its Threads are Tide, Storm and Stone, used practically. Its symbol is a long wake, two lines diverging from a point. Its people are the Factor, who heads it from Lowkeel; factors, who are its agents in every town; flossers, who harvest Knots in crews; wake-captains, who carry the floss south; and hired Weavers, whom it calls hands.

What it wants: floss. The Company sees Knots as crops and Founts as mines, and it is not wrong about the yield. It wants charters where they are convenient and Vigils where they are not, a toll on the Loomroad, Saltheart and the Light bought out of the Greyheron wake-house, and Skyfold bound under its seal before anyone else sits a Vigil there. Underneath, it wants what Varu Longwake wanted when she chartered it: that the Scatter should never be poor again.

How it works: contracts, wages, credit and competence. The Company pays on time, keeps its word to the letter and not a syllable further, buys steads that cannot pay their debts, taxes every cart that leaves Lowkeel, and has a factor in every town with a purse open. Its Heddles are the best in the Marches and its flossers find Knots first. Its crews strip Knots, which is to say they cut the Snag and harvest the floss before the Knot can turn, and a stripped Knot turns hungry, and the steads nearby pay for it.

Founts: assets. The Company binds under Chancery charter where it can and under Vigil where it must, and sees no difference. It holds Lowkeel Eye and the Kiln-camps, and it contests bindings by hiring challengers to sit Unbindings on its behalf, which the Compact regards as the worst thing anyone has yet done with the rite.

Weavers: employees. The Company pays Weavers better than anyone and asks fewer questions; it does not care about seals. Its saying is that a Weaver is a tool that can quit, which is meant as respect.

Seat and people: Lowkeel, on the Saltwrack. Factor Tomaru Longwake, Varu's grandson, shrewd and patient and not cruel, who has never broken a contract and has never signed one he could lose; Marisol Orriaux, the house of Orriaux's agent, who holds the purse; Kelat Tallmast, chief of the flossers, who has cut more Snags than anyone alive and is going deaf from it.

How it sees the others: the Chancery is a partner to be managed; the Compact is a customer that will not admit it; the Menders are a charity it funds; the Loosed are a supplier of Void floss and nothing else.

## The Order of the Mended Thread

What it is: the religious order of the Mending Houses, founded from the company that came back from the Needle-rite. Its Threads are Bloom, Lumen and Mind. Its symbol is a needle. Its people are the Needle, who heads a house; Stitchers, who are its full members and all Weavers; sisters and brothers, who are lay and keep the hospices; and the wardens of the Stitch, who keep the watch-house at the Last Stitch. They are called the Menders by everyone, including themselves.

What it wants: to mend. Thinnings closed, the Tangled healed where they can be healed, wild Founts quieted, the Rule of the Needle taught, and the Last Stitch held. It wants to understand the Fray, which it has watched for two centuries. It wants to keep the Loosed from the Stitch without killing them, and it is not sure this is possible. Underneath, it wants to know whether the Absent is coming back, and has decided to do the work as if the answer is no.

How it works: hospices, pilgrimage and refusal. Every town that will have one has a Hospice-gate, where the Menders heal anyone and ask nothing. Pilgrim-Stitchers walk from Knot to Knot closing what they can. The wardens hold the Stitch. The Order teaches freely, keeps the calendar, buries the dead of every people, and famously refuses to take a side until a side is cruel, at which point it is implacable. It treats Tangled creatures as sick rather than evil, which the Hedge finds exasperating and which has saved more lives than the Hedge admits.

Founts: sacred. The Menders bind Founts only to quiet them, pledge every Welling to healing, and hand quieted Founts to steads that swear the Rule of the Needle. They hold Alderheart and the Hospice. They will not contest a binding for gain, but they will sit an Unbinding against a Holder who is cruel, and have done so, and the Company has not forgotten it.

Weavers: vocations. Any Weaver may become a Stitcher, of any people, sealed or not; the Order's saying is that the Loom does not check your papers. It does not license, it does not pay, and it is never short of Weavers.

Seat and people: the Hospice at Alderheart in the Sallows. Needle Oriane Solenne, old, sharp and kind, who has sat with more dying Tangled than any person living; Stitcher Elouan Mirevault, warden of the Stitch, who has gone a day's walk into the Fray and does not discuss it; Sister Celandine, who keeps the Hospice-gate at Ninewells and sets bones.

How it sees the others: the Chancery is a cousin who became a lawyer; the Compact is the best hope in the Marches and too proud to say so; the Company is money that does some good by accident; the Loosed are the Order's own doubt, walking around outside it.

## The Loosed

What it is: not an order but a movement, made of people who went into the Fray and came back. Its Threads are Void, Umbra and Mind. It has no symbol, though its people leave a cut cord where they have been. They take frayed names: a Loosed Weaver cuts their old name down to a syllable and keeps that. They call themselves the Loosed. The Chancery calls them ravelers.

What it wants: for the Loom to be let go. No bindings, no Lanterns, no seals, no Stitch. The Loosed hold that the Dropped Shuttle was dropped on purpose so that the Loom could weave itself, that the Loosening is the Loom learning, that Founts should be unbound so that they can wake fully, and that a Weaver's Skein is a cage the Weaver carries. They want to go east, and come back, and tell. Underneath, they want to know what the Fray is, and they think they do, and they will not say it plainly, because they say it cannot be said plainly.

How it works: speech, pilgrimage and the rite of Unbinding. The Loosed teach freely and well, which is the dangerous part. They sit Unbindings against bindings they consider cages, including good ones, and an Unbound Fount near a stead breeds Knots, which they say is the Loom waking and the stead says is ruin. They live in camps in the Hush that move. They trade Void floss from the Fray to the Company and do not care what it is used for. They mostly refuse violence, and say that unmaking is not violence, and the Hedge does not agree. Some of them have been changed by the Fray and are Thinnings walking; sound arrives late around them.

Founts: should be unbound. The Loosed sit at the Needle's Eye and do not bind it. They have Unbound Founts that bred Knots under cruel Holders, and been thanked for it, and they have Unbound Founts that fed villages, and been hunted for it.

Weavers: every Weaver is a Loosed who has not noticed yet. The Loosed will teach any Weaver anything and ask only that they come east once to see.

Seat and people: none fixed. They are found at the Last Stitch and in the Hush and in the back rooms of inns. Sev, who was Severin Mirevault, a sealed Lanterner, and who spoke in the Wellhouse for an hour and was not thrown out; Ash, a Marcher girl from Reedham who went east at fourteen and came back at twenty and is the one the Compact fears, because she is theirs; Ond, who does not speak and does not need to.

Why they are not simply villains: they are sometimes right. Their Unbindings have quieted Founts that were breeding Knots under cruel hands. The Compact half agrees with them and is ashamed of it. The Menders' deepest doubt is that the Loosed are the Rule of the Needle carried one step further than the Order dares. And in two centuries of being questioned, no Loosed has yet been caught in a lie.

## Tensions

The Chancery and the Compact are in the Licensing Dispute, and have been since the Marchwarden arrived: seals against the Vigil, Candlehall Font against the Wellmother, the sealed steads against the Moot. It is a cold war fought in writs and boycotts and the occasional fist, and it is the shape of Marcher politics.

The Chancery and the Company are allies by charter and rivals in fact. The Company's charter gives it what the Compact denies the Chancery, and the Chancery suspects, correctly, that the Company bribes its clerks. Both want Skyfold.

The Chancery and the Menders are an old family quarrel. The Chancery keeps the Menders' calendar and burns their candles; the Menders refuse the Chancery's seals and shelter the unsealed. The Menders' tolerance of the Loosed is, to the Chancery, the one thing it cannot forgive.

The Chancery and the Loosed are at war, as far as the Chancery is concerned. The Loosed do not appear to have noticed.

The Compact and the Company quarrel over tolls, charters, Knot-rights, Burls, and flosser crews that strip Knots near steads and leave them hungry. They also need each other: the Company buys the Compact's floss and the Compact eats on the proceeds. The counting-house in Ninewells is the most resented building in the town and nobody has burned it.

The Compact and the Menders are friends who disagree about killing. The Hedge kills the Tangled; the Menders heal what they can; both have buried people over the difference. The Reach of Alderheart overlaps steads that were there first, and the Moot has opinions.

The Compact and the Loosed are fear and sympathy braided. Sev was heard in the Wellhouse. Ash is a Reedham girl. Unbindings near steads breed Knots, and the Hedge has had to cut them. The Compact's secret is that its own creed, the commons and the Vigil, is closer to the Loosed than to anyone else's, and Maud Thatcher knows it.

The Company and the Menders: the Menders denounce stripping from the Hospice-gate, and the Company funds the Hospice, and both go on doing it.

The Company and the Loosed trade. The Company does not care what anyone believes.

The Menders and the Loosed share the Last Stitch, the Needle's Eye and a doubt. Stitchers have gone over. The Order has not yet decided what to do about that, and the Loosed are patient.

And everyone wants Skyfold, which woke in M 303 and is unbound, and sits on the highest tor in the Emberfells answering no one. The first Vigil sat there will be contested by every power in the Marches, and the new Weavers arriving at Ninewells are being measured, by all of them, for exactly that.

## Minor groups

### The Greyseam Delvers

A Durn kin at Scarrow, at the Girdle's foot, who hold the Greyseam Heart and have held it through the tear and since: the oldest continuous binding in the Marches. Seam-master Haldrek Greyseam speaks for them, rarely. They mine Stone and Ember floss, trade it at Scarrow for everything they do not grow, keep stone-silence about the maker and most other things, respect the Compact, distrust the Company, and ignore the Chancery. They want to be left alone and to reopen the deep seams that closed on Ninth Void, and they hire Weavers to clear Delves because the Delves keep opening where the seams were. They have never asked for help and have twice been given it, and they remember both times.

### The Spindlewatch

Hermit-scholars who keep a house at the foot of the Spindle and a smaller one on the Knap road into Ninewells. Watcher Gil Penrose, descendant of Oriel Penrose of the Nine Who Stayed, leads them in the sense that someone has to. They keep the calendar by the Spindle's shadow, declare the sixth Selvage day, measure the Warp's drift in the northern sky, and named the Loosening. They are neutral in everything, sell nothing, answer questions in a way that costs the asker something, and are consulted by every power in the Marches, none of which likes what it is told. What they want is to know what the Spindle is. They have been at it for a long time.

### The Hedgecrows

Outlaws of the Hallows' hedgerows and barrow-fields, led by Kit Tallow, called Crowmother. They rob floss carts, the Company's for preference, poach Knots that steads have claimed, smuggle for Compact steads around the Lowkeel tolls, and harbour unsealed Weavers who have crossed the Chancery. The Hedge hunts them lightly and the Moot pretends not to buy from them. They know the Hobs of the Hallows better than anyone living, because they live in them, and a new Weaver who needs to find a way through a Tangle in the dark will be sent, by someone who will deny it, to the Crowmother.
