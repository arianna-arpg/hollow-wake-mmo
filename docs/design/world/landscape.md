# Landscape

The land itself: where there is land and where there is sea, where the mountains stand, how the water runs, what grows where, what the sky does. The landscape is the field of adventure, so it must read as a place rather than as noise: valleys that lead somewhere, ridges you can follow, rivers you can walk along, coasts with harbors, caves under hills. Everything on this page is a pure function of the seed, the content, the pins and the generation ledger, computed at the scale it belongs to and cached there.

## Scales and plans

Generation runs at five scales, each a plan that the scale below reads. A plan is computed once, lazily, when something first asks for it, from the plans above it and a bounded neighborhood of its own scale, and is cached by coordinate. Plans never read the simulation; the simulation reaches generation only through the generation ledger.

- **The world plan** is the shape of a world: plates, continents, seas, mountain belts, the great rivers' sources, the climate bands. It is computed from the seed alone and the content's world shape, tiled by plate cells so that any point of an endless world is a function of its own cell and a bounded ring of neighbors. The lore's geography is the plan's pins around the origin: the Hem sea to the south, the Wake to the west, the Girdle to the north, the Fray to the east, and the Weftmarches between them; past them the same plan goes on in every direction, generated as it is explored, and what lies beyond the Girdle or across the Wake is whatever the plan puts there.
- **The region plan** adds what needs a region's worth of context: the region's archetype and blends (as today), its hydrology (rivers traced and lakes filled), its belts and valleys, its settlement field and its routes once places exist. Hydrology and routes read a bounded ring of neighboring regions, never the whole world, so a region plan costs the same at any distance from the origin.
- **The zone plan** places the zone's places under budgets and constraints, lays out each place through its grammar, instantiates its quests, and records the layers and instances the zone offers ([places](places.md)).
- **Chunk passes** write cells: terrain from climate and biome, water from the hydrology, roads, caves, landmarks and parts from the place plans, flora, spawns. They are ordered by rule and registered by name ([pipeline](pipeline-and-budgets.md)).
- **Surfaces** are what a cell carries: a base surface and any number of layers, each with a height, walkability, tags and a headroom, so that bridges, floors, ledges, overhangs and caves are all answers to one question: which surface is under me at this height.

## Landmasses and continents

A continent is not a noise threshold; it is a decision of the world plan. The plan tiles the world with plates (a jittered lattice of cells with a seeded velocity each), marks each plate continental or oceanic, and derives from their boundaries the belts where land is pushed up (converging plates), the rifts where it falls (diverging), and the coasts between land and sea. A coarse land mask comes from the plates with the continental noise shaping its edges, so a world has recognizable continents with seas between them and the sea is deep away from the shelf. Region archetypes are picked with this context: a coastal archetype needs a coast nearby, a mountain archetype a belt, an interior archetype neither; the archetype windows by distance from the origin stay as a second axis so difficulty still rises outward. The lore's edges are archetypes in this plan around the origin: the sea, the high mountains and the torn country are traversable and hostile, horizons of the heartland rather than edges of the world. Beyond them the plates go on: other continents, other seas, other belts, each a plate cell's worth of decision made when a player first comes near, so the world has no edge and a second continent needs no code.

## Tectonics and erosion

Mountains come from belts, not from everywhere. Ridge noise is scaled by the belt's strength at a point, so ridges run along belts with foothills on either side and the lowlands between belts are rolling. Erosion is approximated by functions rather than simulated, because a chunk must generate from its own inputs in a bounded time: valleys are carved along the rivers of the region plan with a profile per river size, talus softens the feet of cliffs, and the detail noise is damped on floodplains and sharpened on ridges. The result reads as eroded without any pass depending on an iterative global process.

## Rivers and lakes

Rivers are traced, not found. The region plan picks springs by seed where moisture and elevation favor them, then traces each spring downhill on a coarse grid of elevation samples until it reaches the sea, joins another river, fills a lake, or runs out of its bounded length. A trace that reaches a hollow fills a lake: the hollow is flooded on the coarse grid up to its spill height within a bounded area, and the trace continues from the spill. Where two traces meet, the downstream river's size is the sum of its tributaries. Every trace is a pure function of its spring, and a chunk asks for the traces of the regions within reach of the longest river, so neighboring chunks and neighboring regions agree on every river without any global step. The chunk water pass carves the channel to the river's profile, writes water cells with flow direction and depth, tags banks and fords, and lets lakes set a local water level. The lore's laid rivers, which lie across the land in long deliberate curves, are pinned traces: authored springs and waypoints that the tracer honors.

## Coasts

A coast is where the land mask meets the sea at the water level. The coastal pass shapes it from the slope and the exposure: beaches on gentle slopes, cliffs on steep ones, tidal flats where the shelf is shallow and the tide overlay is wide, dunes behind beaches, salt marsh in the lee of headlands, sea stacks and sea caves where cliffs face the open sea. Harbors are coast places chosen where a bay shelters deep water. Tides are an overlay the world clock drives, so the Saltwrack's flats uncover and drown on a schedule, and a Knot can make them unreliable with an overlay of its own.

## Mountains with real verticality

Height is real: a belt rises to many times a body's height, and a mountain is walked, not painted. Slopes above the walkable limit become cliffs (blocked cells), highland biomes terrace into ledges that must be jumped or climbed, and the road pass lays switchbacks and mountain passes where the route plan crosses a belt. Ledges and overhangs are layers: a cell on a cliff face can carry a walkable ledge above the base surface, and the surface above an overhang is the base with a layer beneath it. Peaks, passes, cols and scree are place kinds that the zone plan sites by slope and elevation, so a mountain has things in it rather than only a shape. The maximum height of a belt is a rule, because fall damage, the cameras and the client's chunk meshes all read it.

## Caves and overhangs

A cave is a surface below the ground with headroom. Within the stacked-surface model a cell's layers can lie under the base as well as over it: a cave floor is a layer with a height below the base and a headroom that says how high the open volume reaches; the base surface above it is tagged as overburden and stays walkable on top. At a cave mouth the base itself drops to the floor, so walking in from outside is ordinary walking, and inside, the ground query finds the floor for a body at floor height and the hill for a body on the hill. Movement respects headroom: a jump stops at the ceiling, and a surface with too little headroom for a body is not standable. The cave pass grows cave systems from mouths the zone plan sites in cliffs and hillsides, as bounded worms with chambers, with the same tools biomes use: windows on the Thread densities (a Delve in the Emberfells, a Hollow in the Sallows), surface tags, flora and spawns of their own. Shallow caves, tunnels and overhangs live in the open-air layer this way; deep caves and dungeons that need more than a few surfaces per cell are interior layers of a place ([places](places.md)).

## Biomes and sub-biomes with transitions

Biomes stay windows over the climate dimensions, the slope and the Thread densities, with the most specific match winning and sub-biomes narrowing their parents. Three things are added. Climate itself gains the world plan's bands, so a world has warm and cold ends and the lore's Hem is warm and the Girdle cold without a region override saying so. Transitions: each biome may declare an ecotone width, and inside it the match is jittered by a seeded noise so borders are ragged rather than ruled, cells on either side of a border carry an `edge` surface tag with the neighboring biome's id, and flora and surface presentation read the tag to blend. Features: a biome may declare sub-biome features (a clearing, a bog, a grove, a thicket, a boulder field) with a density and a size; the feature pass stamps them as seeded blobs inside the biome so the inside of a forest is not uniform. Thread densities keep driving the strange biomes, and overlays keep shifting any biome toward what a decree or an event wants.

## Weather and seasons

The world clock keeps the lore's calendar: nine Spans of forty days, each under a Thread, then the Selvage; a day length in seconds is a rule. Seasons are the calendar's: each Span declares temperature and moisture offsets and the weather kinds it favors, and the world simulation applies them as a global overlay that changes as the Span turns, so the land itself shifts with the year (snow lines fall, meadows brown, the Sallows floods). Weather is a state per region, turned by the world simulation on its clock from the seed and the time, with kinds and transitions as content: clear, overcast, rain, storm, fog, snow, and the Thread-frayed weathers of the lore (ashlight, dead air, upward rain) where a Knot or an event lets them in. A weather state is an overlay with parameters (precipitation, wind, visibility, a surface tag such as wet or snow) and modifiers for entities inside it, and a presentation the clients draw. Weather never changes heights; it changes surfaces, flora, spawns, modifiers and what the sky looks like, and it is replayable: the same seed and the same world time give the same sky.

## Determinism and streaming

- Every value is bit-for-bit reproducible. A fast path may only remove repeated work; the golden tests pin regions, climate, zones, places and chunks, and a package that changes them names the change.
- Every plan is a pure function of the seed, the content, the pins, the generation ledger and the plans it reads; plans read bounded neighborhoods, so cost does not grow with the world or with distance from the origin.
- Cost follows the frontier. Plans are built for the regions players have reached and the ring the world simulation watches around them, held in bounded caches that drop the farthest first, and nothing is built for latent land until a player approaches it or a pin there is read. A world's size in regions is not a cost; its explored size is.
- The generation ledger is exactly four kinds of entry: overlays, chunk edits, place states and pins. The client receives all four (overlays, place states and pins with the world information on entering and as they change, chunk edits with the chunks they touch) and generates everything else itself with the same core, so terrain costs no bandwidth. A client whose content hash differs from the server's does not generate; it requests chunks, as the browser client always does.
- A chunk generates within the chunk budget of [performance.md](../performance.md) from cached plans; the first touch of a plan is amortized and budgeted separately, and the server warms the plans of the zones around players before they arrive.
- Layers and instances generate by the same rules with the layer or instance key folded into every salt.
- Chunk edits apply after generation and survive regeneration: when an overlay changes a chunk's generation, the chunk regenerates and its edits replay on top ([destruction and persistence](destruction-and-persistence.md)).

## Rules used

The `world` group holds sizes and the seed; the `landscape` group holds the world plan's shape, the belt and river profiles, the cave worm limits, ecotone widths, the maximum height, and the calendar's day length; the `weather` group holds the turn interval and the kinds' weights by Span. Numbers never live in code.
