# Items and loot

## Base families

A base item is a family (`short_sword`, `leather_jerkin`) with a class (`weapon.sword`, `armor.chest`, implied as tags), a slot and any slots it also occupies, local value windows (`weapon.damage.min`, `weapon.attack.time`, `weapon.range`), a defense mix priced by the slot budget rule, implicit lines with ranges, requirements, drop weight, minimum level, affix theme affinities and affix caps. Tier resolves from item level through `items.tier_breaks`; tier names prefix the display name; local values scale by `items.local_tier_scale`.

A base may also set its **affix pool law** and a **rarity floor**. Without `affix_pool` the pool is open: every family whose `requires` and `excludes` allow the base may roll. With it the pool is explicit: only the families (or groups) it names roll, whatever their tags, so a relic never rolls a weapon's affix. `min_rarity` raises a rolled rarity below it to it.

## Affixes

An affix family has a kind (prefix or suffix), a group (one per item), name particles by tier, lines (modifier shapes, local or wearer) and tiers best-first with level gates, ranges per line, weights and a magic-only flag. Rolling picks families eligible by item tags, excludes used groups, weights tiers by item level with a bias that favors higher tiers deeper in the ladder, multiplies by theme affinity, and stores one 0..1 roll per line. Values derive at read time, so retuning ranges retunes every existing item. Lines of one part (an affix, the implicits, a unique) that name the same `shared_roll` key read the roll of the first of them, so the two ends of a range rise and fall together.

Rarity definitions set prefix and suffix caps, count distributions and base roll windows, and two contracts. `superior` (commons): with its chance the base roll lands in the top `window` and the name takes rule `items.superior_name`. `overroll` (magic): with its chance an item may roll affix tiers up to `max_steps` above its level gate, each step above weighted down by `step_decay`. Rare names mint from rule tables; magic names from affix particles; uniques pin lines to a base with their own ranges and tier scaling. A unique may roll **choice families**: each family offers options of lines and the mint picks `pick` of them by weight without repeats, kept on the item with their rolls, so two copies differ; `max_per_container` caps how many copies one container board holds.

Every contract added after the first mints (superior, overroll, gear sockets, unique choices) draws from a stream of the item's own seed, not the shared stream, so a shared stream draws what it always drew and an old seed mints the same item.

## Equipment

Equipping checks the slot's accepted tags and the item's requirements, displaces occupants, and recalculates: every `item:` source is removed and re-added from wearer lines; armor, evasion and ward sum from local values; weapon locals become `weapon.*` (main hand) and `offhand.*` bases on the wearer, which is what attack forms read. Displaced items go to the bag, which by then includes the cells the equipped item left; when one of them finds no room the equip is refused and nothing changes, so an item is never lost to a full bag.

**Gear sockets.** Gear mints sockets by rarity: each socket up to the rarity's `max` rolls its `chance` (rules `inventory.sockets.rarity`), bounded by the cap of the item's class, the longest class prefix named in `inventory.sockets.caps` (none named holds none). A socket takes items whose tags meet `inventory.sockets.accepts` (vestige stones); a socketed stone lends the host its lines, local ones to the host's own numbers and the rest to the wearer while the host is worn. Stones go in from the bag (a stone already in the socket goes back to the bag first) and come out to the bag; with no room the move is refused, so nothing is lost. A bench adds sockets up to the cap (station services, below).

## Boards

The bag, every stash page and the reliquary are boards: a grid of cells with an optional mask (rows of `X` and `.`) that switches cells off. An item covers its base's `width` by `height` cells and records its top-left cell on itself, so its place persists with it. The bag's shape is rule `inventory.bag` (`width`, `height`, `mask`), read when a hero enters the world; a saved item keeps its cell while it still fits there, otherwise it takes the first space that fits (row by row from the top left), and an item with no room at all is kept without a cell (shown as an overflow board) until room is made. Nothing merges or is lost on loading.

`InventoryService.Move` is the one move: an item goes to a cell of the bag or a container's board, to an equipment slot, or to the world. On the bag, moving onto exactly one other item swaps the two (the other takes the moved item's old cell, or the first space that fits it); a target under two items, out of bounds or with no room for the swap is refused, and nothing changes. Moves from the equipment doll or a container need free cells at the target. Containers and the weave rack register as places with the service, so a move between any two of them is the same call; a move a place refuses puts the item back on the cell it left.

## Containers and stashes

A **container** (content kind `containers`) is a board beyond the bag: what it accepts (item tags), its owner (the character or the account), whether the items seated in it are active, the interactable kind that reaches it (none reaches it anywhere), whether changing it needs the calm a loadout change needs (rule `casting.swap_requires`), modifiers, and its board as unlock rungs. A rung adds cells (rows of `X` and `.`) when its `unlock` expression holds over the hero and says what it asks as `requirement` text; the board is the union of the unlocked rungs' cells. Rungs are read again when the hero levels, turns in a quest or has a story flag set; items a shrinking board cannot hold wait without a cell until there is room.

The seated items of an **active** container apply their wearer lines to the hero under source `container:{id}`, and while it holds anything the container puts its own modifiers on the hero too; the gauge `container.{id}.cells` counts the cells its items cover, read as `gauge.container.{id}.cells`. The reliquary is the example: the account's board of relics (one cell to two by two), rungs by level, and an experience price per occupied cell that is one modifier line, so removing the line switches the price off. A unique's `max_per_container` holds per board.

A **stash** (content kind `stashes`) is pages of identical boards reached at an interactable of its `at` kind (a stash chest): `free_pages` come with it and the rest are bought one at a time, the next page's price an expression per currency of the hero and the page's number (`page`). Board ids are the container's id, or `{stash}:{page}` for a stash page, and are what a move names.

Boards save with their owners. The character's boards and pages bought ride on its sheet; the account's (the reliquary, the account stash) ride on the sheet as the account's vault while the character plays: the store fills it from the account when the character loads and writes it back to the account when the character saves, so every character of an account shares them, and without an account save they are never written. Loading never loses an item: an item keeps its cell while it fits, takes the first space that fits otherwise, and waits without a cell when there is none; items of a page not owned wait for it; items of a board content no longer has come back in the bag's overflow (the character's) or are kept untouched (the account's).

## Weave items

Heroes own their weaves as items (question Q1 of the parity plan). A **codex** teaches a form, composed with Threads the hero is attuned to, or a whole preset weave, at a level; its rarity sets the sockets its weave slot gets (rule `inventory.codex_sockets`, capped by what the form can hold beside a preset's own glyphs). A **glyph stone** holds a glyph at a level with the row it rolled on each of the glyph's axes. Both are ordinary items with a payload (`ItemInstance.Payload`); one base each (rules `inventory.codex_base` and `inventory.stone_base`), named from rule templates, with payload rollers registered by item class on the item generator.

The **rack** is the weave book's slots: seat `i` holds a learned codex and the stones socketed beside it, and builds weave slot `i` from them (the codex's preset or form at its level, the seat's Threads, the stones in socket order). Learning a codex into an occupied seat swaps the old one back to the bag (a better copy swaps in place); stones stay while the new codex has sockets for them. A stone whose gate fails stays socketed and dormant with its reason (question Q4); the slot compiles without it and keeps casting. Socketing, unsocketing, learning and unlearning check the item's requirements (on the form or glyph) and rule `casting.swap_requires` (question Q11, waived in safe zones); everything that leaves the rack goes back to the bag, all or nothing, and nothing is destroyed. Seats follow their weave slots when the slots are reordered. A seat's nodes of its weave's own tree, bought one at a time with `buy_tree_node` (set-weave semantics: the spec must compile, ownership must allow it, the loadout must be free to change, and a node the compiler would not count is refused with its reason), stay with the seat while it teaches the same weave and save with the character; a different weave learned there starts with none. A loose stone's card lists how it fits each learned seat: it fits, it would sit dormant (with the reason), the seat is full, or it has no sockets.

While rule `casting.require_ownership` holds, a hero's set-weave must name exactly what the seat holds: the codex's preset or form and level, the stones in order with their levels and rows, and attuned Threads (the calling's, and any granted by an attribute `attune.{thread}` above zero). New characters receive their calling's weaves as codices already learned; a save from before the rack gets codices for its weaves once, when it loads. Quest rewards that name weaves give their codices and stones to the bag. Monsters, tools and tests compose freely.

Stones and codices drop from loot entries that name the stone or codex base; an entry may fix the payload (`glyph`, `form`, `weave`), and otherwise one is drawn by the glyphs' and forms' `drop_weight` above their `min_level`, leaning toward the forms and glyphs the dropper weaves with by rule `inventory.drop_lean`. A glyph with **axes** is a chassis: each stone of it rolls one weighted row per axis at mint and keeps it, a copy granted without rows takes the first row of each axis, and the compiler folds the rows' modifiers and joins their mechanism demands to the glyph's gate.

The **preview** a weave's card shows is computed by the server from the same fold a cast uses: each damage effect's lowest and highest roll split over its Threads and folded through the caster's damage with the weave's modifiers, cast time after speed, cooldown after recovery, costs, range, and the weave's own attributes named by their definitions. Labels come from rule `inventory.preview_labels`.

## Loot

Tables hold rolls; each roll draws a count (an expression) of entries by weight: an item (by base or class filter, with rarity or rarity weights and a level bonus), a unique, a nested table, currency with an amount range, or nothing. Conditions on entries read the loot scope. Kills roll the pattern's `drops` table with its chance plus rarity bonus rolls; chests and resource nodes roll tables through interactables. Drops are entities with an owner window and expiry.

## Crafting hooks

Quality on an item scales local values by `items.quality_bonus` and chooses rarity through `crafting.rarity_by_quality`. Bench affix addition reuses the same roll code (`TryAddAffix` with `crafted`).

## Station services

A station sells work as content declares it (kind `station_services`): a registered service `type`, where it is offered (`at`: interactable kinds or station tags), what it takes (`accepts`: item tags), and its price per currency as expressions of the hero and the target (`item_level`, `rarity_rank`, `sockets`, `refunds`). The registered types are `level_item` (a glyph stone or codex rises a level, up to `glyphs.max_level` or `inventory.codex_max_level`; question Q14), `merge_codices` (two copies of a codex, one of them in the bag, become one of the next rarity that gives sockets), `reset_passives` (every bought node back; starts and realm roots stay), `reset_tree` (a weave slot's own tree cleared, its points back, priced by `tree_spent`) and `add_socket` (a socket chiseled into gear up to its cap). A font, a stash chest and a station open the station pane; its state quotes every service for every target it applies to, with the price, whether it can be done now and why not (rule texts `crafting.service_reasons`). Asking names the station whose pane is open and the target; the price is paid all at once and the work is done.
