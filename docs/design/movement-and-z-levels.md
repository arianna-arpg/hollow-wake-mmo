# Movement and z-levels

## Terrain model

A cell has a base surface (height, flags, tags) and may have layers: extra surfaces at other heights (bridges, tower floors, decks). `Terrain.TryFindGround(x, y, z, stepUp)` returns the highest walkable surface not more than `stepUp` above the feet, with the base surface sampled bilinearly for smooth slopes and layers kept flat. That one query resolves standing on a bridge over a river and walking under it.

Cells are blocked by cliffs (slope above `world.gen.max_walkable_slope` in non-terraced biomes), deep water, structure walls, and props flagged blocking (tracked per simulation in `BlockedCells`). Terraced biomes quantise heights to whole steps, which creates ledges you cannot walk up but can jump up.

Cursor picking in both clients intersects visible, loaded base terrain and walkable layer tops. A ray already below a deck can reach the surface beneath it; a plane intersection must lie inside the layer's cell, so entering a deck's footprint below its top does not snap the cursor upward. Missing or unbuilt chunks are not generated by picking. Base terrain keeps its bilinear sampling, and layers stay flat.

A free ground/direction cast treats the submitted Z as a surface-selection hint. The server resolves it to the nearest actual base or walkable layer at the submitted coordinates; it never takes arbitrary airborne Z from that hint. Ties retain the base. Hovered direction input takes its height from the authoritative body's feet and keeps the separately sampled aim offset. Target-kind admission precedes ground sampling. Accepted ground input is range-clamped first; free direction input uses the existing `casting.max_aim_range` bound, matching streamed aim. The bound preserves the planar bearing and the Z surface-selection hint. Streamed aim and release use the same ground resolution. The camera ray is client presentation; the resulting surface height and projectile support are server authority.
## Movement

The movement system integrates per tick: path following or a target or a held direction produce a desired direction; speed is `move.speed`, times the running cast's move factor (set when the cast begins: one for weaves tagged `mobile`, zero for the rest, which roots); stuns and roots lock movement; jumping sets vertical velocity from `move.jump_velocity` scaled by `jump.height` when grounded; gravity is `move.gravity`. Horizontal motion is validated against the destination cell and slides along X or Y when blocked. Vertical motion lands on the ground under the new position and can fall any distance; fall damage is a rule.

Dashes and teleports resolve their destination by marching the segment and stopping at the first cell that cannot be occupied.

Movement keeps the spatial grid current, and everything that asks who is near (perception, targeting, areas, interest) reads it through radius queries. A radius query walks the grid in place without copying: a caller that adds or removes entities, or moves them between grid cells, while enumerating materializes the results first, or the enumeration throws.

Dashes, leaps, blinks and shoves (knockback, pull, buffet) move bodies through the displacement system on top of walking; see [deliveries.md](deliveries.md).

## Pathfinding

A* over cells with eight neighbors, no corner cutting past blocked cells, step-up and max-drop limits, cell costs by tags (roads cheap, bog dear) and a node budget: `move.path_max_nodes` for a player's click-to-move, `move.ai_path_max_nodes` for other movers. The shared move planner first tests a straight walk for both clicks and AI destinations. That test samples at 0.25-unit spacing and takes at most the mover's node budget in samples; a longer segment goes directly to bounded pathfinding. Distant minion orders therefore search in bounded steps instead of testing the entire segment on each renewal.

A destination marked `KeepPlan` can reuse its cache on `Motion` until its path runs out, its goal moves far enough, or it ages past the repath rules (see [AI](ai.md)). A client click always plans afresh. A failed search returns the best partial path and reports whether it reached the goal, got partway or found no step. With no step the mover tries walking directly toward the goal. A partial path is followed to its end; a fresh click stops there. A renewed `KeepPlan` destination can resume walking toward the goal after a partial path that made no progress, without another search until the cache expires.

The pathfinder keeps its search state between searches and can write into the caller's path list, and ground queries resolve stacked surfaces without building anything, so planning and walking allocate nothing in steady state.

The search never stands in a cell a placed thing blocks (the cells BlockedCells tracks, which walking refuses), may pass such a cell's corner on a diagonal as walking does, and counts reaching a cell beside a goal inside one as arriving.
