# Evaluation: tournaments, metrics, bands and trace hashes

Combat is measured, not asserted. The evaluation harness runs seeded duels across pairings of patterns and reports what the simulation's own events say happened: who won and when, what each side dealt, cast and used, and how varied its casting was. Designers keep the pairings and the bands a matched pairing must stay inside as content, gates keep the reports as evidence beside the benchmark rows, and every fight's event log is hashed so a change that should leave the fights alone can show it did.

Code: `src/HollowWake.Core/Evaluation/Tournament.cs` (the runner, the report records and the trace baseline), `src/HollowWake.Tools/TournamentCommand.cs` (the `hollowwake tournament` command). Rules live in group `evaluation`. The recorded baseline is `experiments/combat-grammar/baselines/standard-duels.json`.

## Pairings

Rules group `evaluation` lists the pairings, each written `a:b` with two pattern ids. `proving_pairs` are matched pairings built to be even, the ones a tournament fights unless told otherwise and the ones the bands are for. `standard_duels` are the pairings whose seeded traces are the determinism baseline; `--standard` fights them. `--pairs a:b,c:d` names other pairings for one run and overrides both. Validation refuses a listed pairing not written `a:b` or naming a pattern no definition carries, and a later layer replaces either list like any other rule.

The command checks what it was given before any fight and stops with one line naming the problem: a pairing that names an unknown pattern, an empty `--pairs` entry or one not written `a:b`, a `--seeds` that is not a whole number of at least one, a `--seconds` that is not a positive number, a `--check-hashes` file that is missing or not a baseline, and an `--out` folder that does not exist.

## What a fight is

A fight is one pairing in a fresh arena (`ZoneBuilder.CreateArena`) seeded from the pairing and the fight's index, the two bodies spawned four metres apart under hostile factions (added to the registry only where it lacks them), ticked until one of them dies or the cap in seconds runs out. The fight ends with the tick in which a body died, and the body still standing after that tick wins. When both bodies are dead after it, a double knockout (one blow kills both, or a death throe takes the killer with it), the fight is a draw, like one that reaches the cap with both standing; a double knockout ends before the cap, and like every draw it counts toward the draw rate and not toward the time to kill. Neither side is structurally first. The body spawned first is created first, and so thinks and acts first on every tick the two share, so the side spawned first alternates with the fight's index: a on even indices, b on odd ones. The two spawn mirrored about the arena's origin, a to the west, and each is then turned toward the other, so neither starts facing away (hit angles read facing). `hollowwake fight` stages a duel the same way (`Tournament.Spawn`). Nothing is read from a body beyond its pattern's name: every number comes from `DamageDealt`, `WeaveCast`, `StatusApplied` and `EntityDied`, the mechanisms from the compiled weaves in the caster's book that match the keys it cast. The same seed gives the same fight, so a report repeats exactly and a change in it means a change in content or code.

A side is its body and whatever it owns. Damage, hits, casts and statuses by its minions, constructs, projectiles and fields are credited to it up the owner chain, the way kill credit is (`DeathService.CreditTarget`), so a summoner's kit is measured as its kit. Damage, hits, crits and statuses are a side's work only when the side that owns their target, found up the same chain, is another one (or none). An event whose source and target belong to one side is that side's own doing to itself and is booked apart, per side, as its self-sourced damage and statuses: a buff it lays on itself, a pool of its own breaking and re-arming, and a status that ticks on after its applier is gone, which the simulation then sources from its bearer, so an imp's burn still ticking once the imp is replaced reads as the burned body's own, never as damage that body dealt. Its weaves stay its kit's: the weaves its body cast, and those of its book that anything it owns cast; a minion's own weaves count as casts, not as its owner's kit.

## What a report holds

Per fight: its outcome, when it ended, its trace hash, and per side the damage dealt, hits and crits (a hit is any application of damage that was not evaded, blocked ones included), casts, the distinct weaves cast from its kit and their mechanisms, the size of its kit, the spread of those casts, the statuses applied, its self-sourced damage and statuses, and the fraction of life left. Per pairing: its trace hash, win rates for each side and the draw rate, the median and quartiles of time to kill over the fights that ended, each side's variety (the share of its kit it cast, averaged over fights) and spread (how evenly its casts fell over the weaves it did cast, the entropy of their shares over the most it could be), the union of weaves and mechanisms each side used, each side's self-sourced damage summed over its fights and the self-sourced statuses it bore, and the bands it broke. Per run: a trace hash over its pairings. The whole report serializes to JSON (`--out`), so a pull request can keep it under the evidence folder. Every number the harness writes, in the table, the breaches, the trace and the seeds, is written in the invariant culture by the library itself.

## Bands

Rules group `evaluation` holds the bands a matched pairing must stay inside: `ttk_min` and `ttk_max` for the median time to kill, `win_rate_ceiling` for either side's share of wins, `draw_ceiling` for the share of fights that end in a draw, and `variety_floor` for the share of its kit each side must use. With `--bands` the command names every breach and fails. Bands are for pairings built to be even, such as the proving pairs; an uneven pairing is measured but not banded.

## Trace hashes

A fight's hash is the 64-bit FNV-1a hash (`Rng.Hash64`) of its ordered event log, written as sixteen hex digits. The log holds one line per event in the order the simulation published it, each opening with its tick: every `EntitySpawned` (what and where, the two bodies included) and `EntityDespawned`, every `WeaveCast` (caster, weave, target, aimed point), every `WeaveReleased` (caster, weave, mode, reason, whether it fired, held, fill, stage) and `WeaveInterrupted` (caster, weave), every `DamageDealt` (source, target, amount, and whether it was a crit, evaded or blocked), every `Healed` (source, target, resource, amount), every `StatusApplied` (source, target, status, stacks, duration) and every `EntityDied` (body, killer), then a last line with the outcome and each body's life and position. A fight that reaches the cap with both standing still moves its hash when they end it differently, by regeneration, leech or a step: whatever changes a body's life or place shows in that line. The two bodies are named `a` and `b`; anything else is named by its pattern, or its name when it has none (a projectile, a field), marked with the side that owns it. So the hash follows what the fight did, not how the world numbered its entities. Amounts, durations and positions are written to hundredths in the invariant culture, so the same fight gives the same hash on every machine.

A pairing's hash folds its fights' hashes in seed order, and a run's folds its pairings' in the order they ran. A pairing's hash depends only on its patterns, the content, the code, the number of seeds and the cap, never on what else ran in the same process.

## The determinism baseline

The standard duels' hashes are recorded in `experiments/combat-grammar/baselines/standard-duels.json`: the seeds and the cap they were fought with, and each pairing's hash keyed `a:b`, sorted so a review can diff it. `--hashes` prints the hashes after the table, and with `--out` writes them in that shape instead of the full report. `--check-hashes FILE` fights the selected pairings against such a file, with its seeds and cap unless the command names others, then prints `hashes: identical` or one line per difference (a pairing whose hash changed, one the file does not hold, one it holds that the run did not fight, a different number of seeds or cap) and fails.

A change that should not move a fight checks the standard duels against the baseline and stays identical. A change that does move one regenerates the file in the same pull request and names the change, and the pairings it moved, in its review record. The baseline was recorded on Windows x64; a run on another platform, Linux among them, that differs is a finding to record, not a reason to overwrite the file.

## Using it

```bash
dotnet run --project src/HollowWake.Tools -- tournament                                   # the proving pairs, ten seeds, sixty seconds
dotnet run --project src/HollowWake.Tools -- tournament --pairs wolf:hob,proving_reaver:proving_bulwark --seeds 20 --seconds 45 --bands --out report.json
dotnet run --project src/HollowWake.Tools -- tournament --standard --seeds 3 --seconds 60 --hashes --out experiments/combat-grammar/baselines/standard-duels.json
dotnet run --project src/HollowWake.Tools -- tournament --standard --check-hashes experiments/combat-grammar/baselines/standard-duels.json
```

A gate on a content or AI change runs the proving pairs with bands, keeps the JSON, and compares the mechanisms each side used before and after: a doctrine or a weave that never shows in a report is not proven to work. The report is not a playtest: a human rubric and recorded sessions say whether the fights are good, the harness only says whether they stay inside what the designers wrote down.
